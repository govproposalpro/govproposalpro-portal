# GovProposalPro Proposal Playbook (AI reference)

Read this before drafting any federal proposal or quote. The sources behind it are listed in `proposal-examples.md`. It is written for small-business primes bidding services (janitorial, grounds, facilities, logistics, IT, staffing) under FAR Part 13 (RFQ) or Part 15 (RFP), including set-asides.

## 0. Prime directives
1. **Compliance first, persuasion second.** If the proposal is non-compliant, it never gets scored. GAO consistently upholds exclusions for missing elements, a wrong format, or exceeded page limits (Quarles B-251095; CMC B-290152).
2. **Never parrot the PWS.** Restating requirements is grounds for an unacceptable rating (Bannum B-271075; Res Rei B-410466.7). Every requirement gets the **how, who, when, how often, with what, and how verified.**
3. **The offeror bears the burden.** "A vendor is responsible for affirmatively demonstrating the merits of its quotation" (GAO, Reagent World B-415490). Evaluators may not assume facts that are missing from the proposal.
4. **Follow the solicitation over this playbook.** Its instructions, headings, page limits and order govern. The FAR is being rewritten (Revolutionary FAR Overhaul, 2025–2026), so clause numbers and the "Section L/M" labels may differ. Map to whatever the solicitation calls them.
5. **Be truthful.** Never invent past performance, certifications, staff, or numbers. Where user data is missing, insert a bracketed placeholder `[CONFIRM: ...]` and do not guess.

## 1. Structure

### 1a. RFQ / simplified acquisition (FAR Part 13, commercial; usually 5–20 pages total)
Use the RFQ's requested order exactly. When it gives none, use this order:
1. **Cover letter** (1 page; see §10).
2. **Quote summary / executive summary** (½–1 page): the need restated in the customer's terms, the offer in one sentence, 2–3 win themes, and the price total if allowed.
3. **Technical capability / work plan:** an approach organized by PWS paragraph; staffing (positions, hours, shifts); schedule and frequencies; equipment and supplies; the key person or supervisor and their qualifications.
4. **Quality control plan summary:** an inspection table tied to the PRS/AQLs, corrective action, and complaint response time.
5. **Past performance:** 2–3 recent, relevant references with contract number, value, period, scope, and POC (FAR 52.212-1(b)(10)).
6. **Price:** the completed schedule/CLINs exactly as provided, plus a short price narrative if allowed.
7. **Administrative items:** SAM/UEI/CAGE, acknowledgment of amendments, reps and certs (52.212-3 via SAM, plus any fill-ins), and a statement of agreement with terms if not using the SF 1449 (52.212-1(b)(11)).

Short RFQs still need requirement-specific content. A capability statement alone fails (Reagent World).

### 1b. Full RFP (FAR Part 15, multiple volumes)
- **Vol I Technical:** executive summary; technical approach by PWS area; staffing and workforce management; QC plan; phase-in/transition; safety/environmental (a separate factor if Section M lists it).
- **Vol II Management:** organization chart; key personnel and résumés with letters of intent; subcontract management; communications with the CO/COR; risk management.
- **Vol III Past Performance:** reference write-ups, PPQs sent to references (if required), and the relevance mapping.
- **Vol IV Price:** completed schedule, price narrative, wage determination compliance, assumptions (only where permitted).
- **Vol V Contract documents:** SF 33/1449 signed, amendments, reps and certs, small business subcontracting plan (large business only), and any required forms.
Mirror Section L's volume and paragraph numbering **exactly**, with one proposal heading per L paragraph. Keep information in the volume the RFP assigns it to. CMC lost partly because "the proposal's content jumped from one factor's information to another."

## 2. Scoring against Section L/M

### 2a. Compliance matrix (build it before writing)
Use one row per "shall/must/will/provide/describe/demonstrate" found in L, M, the PWS, and the attachments:
| Req ID | Source (L.x / M.x / PWS x.x) | Requirement | Proposal section & page | Evaluation factor | Status |
Include an abbreviated version in the proposal when the RFP requires or allows it (Quarles needed a PWS cross-reference index). Do this even when it is optional, if page count permits.

### 2b. Write to the evaluator's worksheet
Evaluators document **strengths, significant strengths, weaknesses, significant weaknesses, deficiencies, and uncertainties** (FAR 15.001, FAR 15.305; DoD Source Selection Procedures 2022).
- **Strength** = an aspect with merit or that exceeds a requirement in a way that is advantageous to the Government. To earn one: state the requirement, state how you exceed it, and quantify the benefit to the agency.
- **Weakness** = "any flaw in the proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance" (FAR 15.001). Pre-empt each obvious risk with a mitigation.
- **Deficiency** = a material failure to meet a requirement. This usually comes from an omission.
- **Uncertainty** = vague language. Replace "as needed" with "within 2 hours of notification."
- **LPTA:** Strengths earn nothing. Write to be unambiguously *acceptable* on every element, with no extras that add price.
- **Best value / tradeoff:** Pack in documentable strengths, especially where M says a factor is "significantly more important."

### 2c. Features → benefits → proof (FBP)
Every claim follows this pattern: **Feature** (what we do) → **Benefit** (what the agency gets, ideally measured) → **Proof** (where we've done it, a metric, a certification, a named person).
Example: "Electronic QC inspections with photo evidence (feature) give the COR same-day visibility into every deficiency and its fix (benefit). On the [X] contract this held a 98.6% pass rate over 14 months (proof)."

### 2d. Discriminators
A discriminator is something that is true of us, important to this customer, and not true of the likely competitors. Use 2–4 per proposal and repeat them in the executive summary, the relevant sections, and the cover letter. Ghost competitor weaknesses without naming anyone ("Unlike approaches that rely on hiring the incumbent workforce alone, we...").

### 2e. Proof points bank
Use specific numbers: square feet serviced, years, retention %, inspection pass rate, response times, CPARS ratings, certifications (ISSA CIMS, OSHA 30, Green Seal), and named key personnel with years of experience.

## 3. Win themes and executive summary
- **Win theme formula:** [Customer hot button] + [our discriminator] + [quantified benefit]. Example: "Zero service gaps on day one: a 30-day phase-in with a fully badged crew by day 25 means the agency's facilities stay clean through the turnover."
- **Executive summary pattern (½–2 pages):**
  1. The customer's mission and need in their own words (cite PWS objectives).
  2. Our solution in one or two sentences.
  3. 3–4 themes, each with proof, ideally in a small table: Need | Our approach | Benefit.
  4. Risk reduction (transition, staffing, QC).
  5. A commitment statement with a single accountable person by name.
- Lead with the customer, not the company history. Keep "We are pleased to submit" boilerplate to the cover letter.
- Use active voice, short sentences, and the agency's terminology. Bold one theme sentence per major section.

## 4. Technical approach for service contracts

### 4a. Organize by PWS
Follow the PWS paragraph order. For each task, cover: method/procedure, frequency, staff assigned, equipment/chemicals, standard met, and how it is verified. Address *every* task, including low-visibility ones such as GFE care, 24/7 coverage, reporting, and key control (AHNTECH lost on omitted PWS sections).

### 4b. Staffing plan
- Present a table of positions with: headcount, FTEs, shift, hours/day, days/week, and SCA labor category matching the WD.
- **Justify the hours bottom-up:** cleanable square footage ÷ production rate (for example, ISSA 612 Cleaning Times), plus periodic tasks, plus supervision. Tie the total to the price. Evaluators may judge adequacy by function, and an unexplained low FTE count invites a weakness (NRDC B-409617.3).
- Split full-time from part-time and assign each to a shift (required in CMC).
- **Key personnel:** name them, list their qualifications against the RFP minimums, and attach letters of intent if they are not employees.
- **Recruiting and retention:** sources, a pre-qualified candidate pool, the background-check/badging timeline, and backup staffing (floaters, on-call pool).
- **Incumbent workforce:** Interviewing incumbents is good practice. Never make it the *only* plan. If you claim incumbent retention, supply evidence (letters of intent) and a fallback recruitment plan (AHNTECH). Note that the EO 14055 right-of-first-refusal was revoked in 2025, so only reference FAR 52.222-17 if it appears in the solicitation.

### 4c. Work schedule
Provide a frequency matrix (daily, weekly, monthly, quarterly, annual) by area type, with service windows that respect facility hours and security escort rules. Include a periodic-services calendar (floor strip/wax, carpet extraction, windows) and say how you will coordinate it with the COR in advance.

### 4d. Quality control plan (QCP)
- **Mirror the PRS/QASP line by line.** For each performance objective: the standard, the AQL from the PRS, our internal target (stricter than the AQL), inspection method, frequency, inspector, and record.
  Example rows: "Restrooms clean and stocked | PRS AQL 95% | target 98% | supervisor inspection of 100% of restrooms daily, QC manager random 10% weekly | electronic checklist with photos."
- **QC organization:** a QC manager who is independent of the production supervisors where size allows, with stated qualifications.
- **Inspection system:** scheduled plus unannounced inspections, checklists, and scoring.
- **Corrective action:** the deficiency is logged, then corrected within X hours, then root cause identified, then retraining, then re-inspection. Repeat failures escalate to the PM.
- **Customer complaints:** intake channels, acknowledgment time (for example, within 1 hour), resolution time, and a closed-loop report to the COR. CMC was rated unacceptable for giving only "general representations" here.
- **Reporting:** a monthly QC report to the COR with trend data, and joint inspections with the COR on request.
- **Contractor QC vs government QA:** We perform QC. The COR performs QA under the QASP (FAR 46.4). Offer to share our data so QA is easier.

### 4e. Transition / phase-in plan (often its own element; see Leumas)
Provide a timeline from award to full performance: kickoff with the CO/COR (days 1–3); badging and background checks submitted (days 1–10); hiring and training (days 5–25); supplies and equipment staged; walkthrough with the outgoing contractor and COR; baseline condition inspection documented; day-1 readiness checklist. Define deliverables such as the QCP and work plan "within 10 days of award" when the RFQ requires them (USDA FS example). Name who owns each step.

### 4f. Risk mitigation
Use a table of Risk | Likelihood/Impact | Mitigation | Owner. Typical risks: staffing shortfall or turnover; badge delays; surge or emergency requests; supply chain; weather (grounds); subcontractor non-performance; security incidents. Each needs a concrete mitigation, never "we will monitor closely."

### 4g. Safety and environmental
Cover the site safety plan, OSHA training, SDS/HazCom, PPE, incident reporting to the COR within X hours, green/EPA Safer Choice products where the PWS requests them, and waste segregation.

## 5. Management approach when the prime subcontracts

### 5a. Show the prime is in control (size and responsibility risk)
- The prime provides the **Program/Project Manager** and the **single point of contact** for the CO/COR, owns QC, and holds the contract relationship. Subcontractors never interface directly with the CO on contractual matters.
- Use an organization chart with clear reporting lines: CO/COR, then the prime PM, then the prime QC manager and site supervisors, then subcontractor crews. Mark which roles are prime employees.
- Oversight mechanisms: written subcontract with flow-down clauses (SCA, safety, security, 52.219-14 reporting); weekly sub performance reviews; prime-run QC inspections of sub work; the prime's right to direct removal or replacement of sub personnel; payment tied to QC results.
- Avoid the **ostensible subcontractor** problem (13 CFR 121.103(h)). Do not let the narrative show the sub performing the "primary and vital" work or managing the contract, and do not rely solely on the sub's experience and personnel.

### 5b. Limitations on subcontracting (FAR 52.219-14; 13 CFR 125.6)
- Services: the prime will not pay more than **50% of the amount paid by the Government** to subcontractors that are not similarly situated. Work a similarly situated sub further subcontracts counts against the prime.
- A proposal that **on its face** exceeds the limit is technically unacceptable (Orincon B-276704; Addx B-404888). Make the price allocation and staffing tables consistent with compliance.
- Compliance statement template (adapt to the facts):
  > "[Company], a [status] small business, will comply with FAR 52.219-14, Limitations on Subcontracting. For the services portion of this contract, [Company] will not pay more than 50 percent of the amount paid by the Government to subcontractors that are not similarly situated entities. [Sub name], a [same status] small business under NAICS [code], is a similarly situated entity, and its share counts toward [Company]'s performance. Our planned allocation is [Company] self-performed [X]%, similarly situated subcontractors [Y]%, other subcontractors [Z]% (Z ≤ 50%). We will track compliance monthly and, as required by paragraph (f), measure it by the end of the base period and each option period."
- If the user's model is to subcontract nearly all labor to non-similarly situated firms on a set-aside, **flag the compliance risk to the user** and do not write around it.

### 5c. Communication with the CO/COR
Cover the kickoff meeting; a monthly status report (staffing, QC results, issues, upcoming periodic work); weekly check-ins during phase-in; an escalation ladder with response times (supervisor 1 hr, PM 4 hrs, executive 24 hrs); a 24/7 emergency number; and the rule that only the CO can change scope (no constructive changes).

## 6. Past performance when the company is new
- **Read the RFP language first.** If it says key personnel, predecessor, or subcontractor records "will be considered," use them fully. If it says only the offeror's own contracts count, do not pad with sub references; state that you have no relevant record and expect a **neutral** rating (FAR 15.305(a)(2)(iv)). A neutral rating is not a negative one.
- Acceptable honest sources, in order: (1) the prime's own federal, state, local, or commercial contracts, even small ones; (2) a joint venture or mentor-protégé partner's record, where allowed; (3) a **similarly situated** or major subcontractor's record, clearly labeled with the work that firm will perform here; (4) key personnel's prior work at other employers, labeled as individual experience ("Ms. X managed the 220,000 sq ft custodial contract at [Agency] for [Former Employer] 2019–2024").
- Never present a sub's or employee's former employer's contract as the offeror's own. Mislabeled past performance damages credibility and can be a false statement.
- **Relevance mapping:** For each reference, show a table comparing this RFP with the reference on scope, size (sq ft, FTEs, $/yr), complexity, contract type, and period. GAO upheld agencies that discount sub records when the prime performs most of the work (Triad B-296511.3), so explain the prime's role on the new contract.
- Write each reference GSA-MAS style: client and contract number; period and value; scope; our role; challenges and how we solved them; results with metrics; POC. Send PPQs if the RFP requires it (CMC missed this).

## 7. Price narrative patterns
- Fill in the government's price schedule **exactly** (every CLIN, option year, unit, and extension). Never alter quantities. If any narrative is allowed, keep it factual:
  - **Basis of estimate:** labor hours from the staffing model × SCA WD rates for the cited WD number and revision, plus fringe/H&W, plus payroll taxes and insurance, plus supplies and equipment, plus overhead/G&A and profit. Show that each labor category is at least its WD rate.
  - **Option-year escalation** and how WD revisions are handled (per FAR 52.222-43 price adjustment).
  - **Price realism** (when M announces it): show that hours match the technical staffing table and that wages meet the SCA. Inconsistent hours between volumes is a classic weakness.
  - **Assumptions:** only if the RFQ permits them. Unpermitted exceptions can make an offer unacceptable.
- Never take exception to terms in the price volume. Keep the price consistent with the subcontracting percentages in §5b.

## 8. Why proposals lose: pre-submission checklist
- [ ] A required element of L, a PWS task, or an attachment is missing (Quarles, CMC, AHNTECH).
- [ ] The text parrots or paraphrases the PWS with no method (Bannum, Res Rei).
- [ ] A generic capability statement or reused proposal is submitted instead of a tailored response (Reagent World, Bannum).
- [ ] Page limit exceeded, wrong font or margins, or wrong volume placement (pages over the limit may be ignored).
- [ ] Staffing is not justified, or hours do not match the price (NRDC, VT Griffin).
- [ ] Reliance on the incumbent workforce with no evidence or fallback (AHNTECH).
- [ ] No key-personnel letters of intent, or qualifications not shown against the minimums (CMC).
- [ ] The QC plan does not mirror the PRS, gives no inspection frequencies, or offers only generic customer-service promises (CMC).
- [ ] Required past-performance questionnaires not sent; relevance not explained.
- [ ] Limitations on subcontracting violated on the face of the proposal (Orincon, Addx).
- [ ] Blanket "we will comply" offers without demonstrating how (1989 GAO janitorial decisions, e.g. B-235661).
- [ ] Amendments not acknowledged; reps and certs incomplete; SAM inactive; NAICS size status wrong.
- [ ] Late submission. Electronic offers must be in by 5:00 p.m. one working day before the deadline under 52.212-1(f)(2)(i)(A). GAO does not apply the "government control" exception to email (Sea Box, VERSA). Submit at least 24 hours early and confirm receipt.
- [ ] Exceptions or assumptions that conflict with the solicitation terms.
- [ ] Inconsistent names, numbers, or facility data across volumes (signals a cut-and-paste job).

## 9. Example paragraphs (janitorial/facilities RFQ; original, for style)
Placeholders: `[Company]`, `[Agency]`, `[Facility]`.

### 9a. Executive summary
1. "[Agency] needs [Facility]'s 48,000 square feet cleaned to a 95% acceptable quality level every night without disrupting daytime operations or security. [Company] will deliver that with a dedicated night crew of six, a full-time on-site supervisor, and a quality system that inspects every restroom every night and reports results to the COR each week."
2. "Our approach rests on three commitments: a fully cleared and trained crew on day one, internal quality targets set above the contract's AQLs, and a single accountable project manager, [Name], who answers the COR's calls within one hour, 24 hours a day."
3. "We reduce [Agency]'s transition risk by submitting all badging packages within five days of award and keeping a trained, pre-screened alternate pool of three custodians so absences never become missed service."

### 9b. Technical approach
1. "PWS 3.2, Restrooms (daily). Custodians clean and disinfect fixtures, partitions, and touchpoints with an EPA-registered disinfectant at its labeled dwell time. They restock dispensers to full and log each restroom on a tablet checklist with a timestamp and photo. Restrooms in Building A are serviced first, by 6:30 p.m., because they close earliest."
2. "Staffing is built from the work, not from a headcount guess. Using ISSA production rates for 31,500 sq ft of office space, 9,050 sq ft of carpet, and 22 restroom fixtures, plus weekly and periodic tasks, the nightly requirement is 44 labor hours. We staff 48 hours (6 custodians × 8 hours) and keep the 4-hour margin for surge and special requests."
3. "Floors are stripped and refinished quarterly, and carpets are hot-water extracted semiannually. The schedule is submitted to the COR 14 days in advance, and work is done on weekends to avoid lobby closures during business hours."

### 9c. Quality control
1. "Our QC Manager, [Name], who does not supervise production staff, conducts unannounced inspections of a random 10% of all cleanable areas each week in addition to the supervisor's nightly 100% restroom check. Each PRS item has an internal target of 98%, three points above the contract's 95% AQL, so we correct drift before it becomes a COR finding."
2. "Every deficiency, whether found by us or reported by the COR, is logged, corrected within four hours (or before 7:00 a.m. for overnight findings), and closed only after re-inspection. Two repeat deficiencies in the same area within 30 days trigger a root-cause review and documented retraining."
3. "The COR receives a monthly QC report showing inspection counts, pass rates by PRS item, complaints and closure times, and corrective actions. We invite the COR to join any inspection."

### 9d. Management and subcontracting
1. "[Company] is the single point of contact for all contract matters. Project Manager [Name], a [Company] employee, directs all on-site work, including work performed by our similarly situated teaming partner, [Sub], and is the only person authorized to coordinate with the COR on scope or schedule."
2. "[Sub] performs specialty floor care under a written subcontract that flows down the Service Contract Act, safety, and security requirements. [Company]'s QC Manager inspects [Sub]'s work against the same checklist as our own crews, and payment to [Sub] is released only after acceptance."
3. "[Company] will comply with FAR 52.219-14. Our planned allocation of the amount paid by the Government is 62% self-performed, 18% to similarly situated [Sub], and 20% to other subcontractors for supplies-related services, and we will verify it at the end of each performance period."

### 9e. Past performance (new firm, honest framing)
1. "[Company] was formed in 2024 and is performing its first commercial custodial contract, a 22,000 sq ft medical office building for [Client] (Jan 2025–present, $186,000/yr), with no missed service nights to date."
2. "Our Project Manager, [Name], brings eleven years of federal custodial management *at prior employers*. This includes managing a 140,000 sq ft GSA facility contract for [Former Employer] from 2019 to 2024, which received CPARS ratings of Very Good. We present this as individual experience, not as [Company]'s corporate record."
3. "[Sub], our similarly situated teaming partner, will perform the floor-care tasks described in PWS 3.6. [Sub] has performed the same tasks for [Agency 2] under contract [number] since 2021 (POC: [name, phone])."

### 9f. Price narrative
1. "Prices are built from 48 nightly labor hours at Wage Determination [No.] Rev. [X] rates for Janitor (11150) and Working Supervisor, plus the WD health and welfare rate, vacation, and holidays. No labor category is priced below its WD minimum."
2. "Option-year prices include [X]% escalation for supplies. Labor adjustments for new WD revisions will be requested only as provided by FAR 52.222-43."

## 10. Formatting and administrative conventions
- **Page limits:** Respect them absolutely. Cover pages, tables of contents, résumés, and the compliance matrix count unless excluded. If unspecified, aim for an RFQ technical volume of 5–15 pages.
- **Fonts and layout:** Use the RFP's specification. The common default is 12-pt Times New Roman or similar, 10-pt minimum in tables, 1-inch margins, and 8.5×11 pages. Number pages and use headers and footers: "[Company] | Solicitation [No.] | Volume [X] | Page n of N". Mark proprietary pages with the FAR 52.215-1(e) legend: "This proposal includes data that shall not be disclosed outside the Government..." (use the full required wording).
- **Headings:** Mirror L numbering (for example, "L.4.2.1 Staffing Plan"). Use tables for staffing, schedules, QC, and risk. Keep bold theme statements to one per section.
- **Cover letter (1 page):** date; CO name, agency, and address; solicitation number and title; company legal name, address, **UEI**, **CAGE**, and TIN where requested; small business status claimed under the NAICS code and size standard (for example, SDVOSB/VetCert, WOSB, 8(a), HUBZone); acknowledgment of all amendments by number and date; offer validity period (as specified, often 60–120 days); statement of agreement with all terms (no exceptions); a one-sentence value statement; and the authorized signatory with name, title, phone, and email.
- **Registration and reps:** Confirm an active **SAM.gov** registration (FAR 52.204-7) with current 52.212-3 reps and certs and the correct NAICS. Complete any solicitation-specific fill-ins (for example, 52.204-24/26 telecom, 52.209-11 tax/felony, 52.219-1 small business program reps if not in SAM). Check that set-aside certifications are current (SBA certify.sba.gov for 8(a), HUBZone, WOSB; VetCert for SDVOSB).
- **Submission:** Use the exact method (email, PIEE, SAM, agency portal) and naming convention, keep files under the size limit, and use the requested format (PDF or Excel for price). Submit early, then confirm receipt in writing.
- **Final quality check:** spell-check the agency, facility, and solicitation numbers; keep numbers consistent across volumes; make sure all `[CONFIRM: ...]` placeholders are resolved or flagged to the user.

## House model: a supply quote for a brand-name-or-equal RFQ (from a Contract Bros example)

Use this structure when the solicitation is a combined synopsis/solicitation or RFQ for commercial products
(FAR Part 12/13), unless its instructions say otherwise. It is the format the Contract Bros team uses and wins with.

1. **Cover page.** Title of the requirement, solicitation number and response date at top. Left: "SUBMITTED TO" block
   copied from the solicitation's point of contact (title, name, email, agency, contracting office, full address).
   Then "OFFEROR" block: company, owner name and title, address, phone, UEI, CAGE, TIN if the member provided it,
   email, website, and "Business Size and Type" (e.g. SDVOSB, minority-owned). Product or site photos may go on the right.
2. **Table of contents** listing every part.
3. **Part 1: Executive Summary.** 1.1 Proposal Overview: one paragraph naming the solicitation number, the
   facility, the quantity and exact product offered, and that it meets the salient characteristics.
   1.2 Compliance Statement: a numbered list, each item bolded: Set-Aside Requirements (status and SAM registration),
   Product Specifications (sub-bullets restating each salient characteristic), Authorized Dealer Status, Warranty
   Terms, No Gray Market Items, Technical Description and Pricing Information, Representations and Certifications
   (FAR 52.212-3 completed in SAM), Amendments (acknowledged).
4. **Part 2: Technical Proposal: Description of Offered Items.** Product name and quantity as a heading;
   2.1 Product Literature and Specifications: one bolded bullet per characteristic, worded to match the
   solicitation's salient characteristic; 2.2 Warranty Terms and Conditions: coverage period, inclusions,
   exclusions, claims process.
5. **Part 3: Price Proposal.** Solicitation number, offeror, date of submission; a bordered table with Item
   Description, Unit Price, Quantity, Total Price; Subtotal; Shipping and Handling; Total; then a short Total Cost Summary.
6. **Part 4: Relevant Past Performance.** Numbered projects, each with Scope, Elements Provided, Outcome.
   4.1 List of Similar Contracts (contract number, schedule or vehicle, term, scope). 4.2 Contact Information for
   References (organization, project, contact person, address, phone). If the work was done with a manufacturer or
   teaming partner, say so plainly ("in collaboration with...").
7. **Acknowledgment of Amendments.** Solicitation number, offeror, date; table of Amendment Number, Date Received,
   Acknowledgment Date; for each amendment a summary of changes (revised specifications, Q&A, new deadline) and a
   compliance statement; signature block.
8. **One statement per clause that requires one**, each as its own part with solicitation number, offeror and date:
   e.g. "Statement of Compliance with FAR 52.211-6 Brand Name or Equal" (brand name, proposed product, each salient
   characteristic met, documentation and verification, warranty, authorized dealer) and "Statement on
   Non-Acceptance of Remanufactured or Gray Market Items" under VAAR 852.212-71 (new OEM items only, definition,
   authorized dealer verification, warranty and support). End each with "By signing below, I certify..." and a
   signature block (name, title).
9. **Appendix A: Technical Data Sheets** per item (description, dimensions, materials, features, compliance,
   warranty, installation) and a shipping and handling sheet (method, FOB terms, cost, packaging, delivery address,
   delivery schedule). **Appendix B: Copies of Relevant Certifications and Authorizations** (e.g. SBA certification
   with entity, date, number and validity; OEM authorization or country-of-origin letters).

Lessons from it: restate the government's exact words for every salient characteristic; keep compliance items
numbered so evaluators can check them off; acknowledge every amendment in its own table; and put each required
certification on its own signed page.
